## The short answer

**Under the GHG Protocol, emissions from fuel you burn on-site sit in Scope 1. Emissions from electricity you buy from the grid sit in Scope 2.** A factory running a captive gas generator is burning fuel on-site — so that share of its power supply is Scope 1, full stop, regardless of how the rest of the facility is powered.

## Why this matters more in South Asia than the GHG Protocol's default examples assume

Most GHG Protocol worked examples assume a facility bought from the grid. That is not the typical case for industrial operations in Bangladesh, where grid reliability drives many factories to run **captive natural gas generation** for a meaningful share of baseload power, with grid electricity supplementing rather than leading.

The practical effect: a factory that looks "efficient" on a per-unit-grid-electricity basis can still carry a large Scope 1 footprint that a Scope-2-only analysis would miss entirely.

## What changes because of this

**Measurement changes.** You need combustion analysis and fuel-flow data for the generator fleet, not just a utility bill. A power quality analyser or a smart meter on the grid connection tells you nothing about the generator's fuel-to-output ratio.

**The reduction strategy changes.** Scope 2 reduction usually means switching electricity supply — a renewable power purchase agreement, or grid decarbonisation outside your control. Scope 1 reduction from captive generation means combustion efficiency, generator maintenance and load-matching, waste heat recovery, or a genuine fuel switch — decisions inside the factory's own control, with different capital and payback profiles.

**The reporting changes.** A buyer questionnaire that assumes "Scope 2 = your electricity" and asks nothing about on-site generation will misclassify your footprint if you answer it literally. Getting this right in your own inventory protects you when the buyer's own analysts cross-check it later.

## How to check where you stand

If your facility runs any on-site generation — gas, diesel, or otherwise — ask two questions before assuming your Scope split looks like a textbook example: what share of baseload power does the generator fleet actually carry, and has anyone measured its combustion efficiency directly rather than estimating it from fuel purchase records.
