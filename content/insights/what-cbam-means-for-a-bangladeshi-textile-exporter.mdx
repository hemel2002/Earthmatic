## What is CBAM, in one paragraph?

The EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) charges importers for the embedded emissions in certain goods brought into the EU, priced against the EU carbon market. It currently covers iron and steel, cement, aluminium, fertilisers, hydrogen and electricity — not finished garments. **A Bangladeshi RMG exporter is not a direct CBAM filer today.**

## So why does a garment factory need to care?

Two reasons, and neither depends on the product list expanding.

**Trims and inputs already fall inside scope.** Steel components, aluminium fixtures and other CBAM-covered inputs used in your supply chain carry embedded-emissions reporting obligations upstream, and your buyers are starting to ask for that data as part of broader supply-chain due diligence.

**Buyers are pricing in the direction of travel, not just the current rule.** European and North American brands are building supplier-level emissions data collection now, ahead of any garment-specific CBAM expansion, because the EU's own review clauses signal the product list will grow. A supplier who can already produce verified Scope 1–3 data is simply easier to keep on the vendor list.

## What "verified supplier-level data" actually requires

Estimates and industry averages do not clear the bar buyers are now setting. What does:

- A **Scope 1 and 2 inventory per facility**, not a company-wide average
- **Scope 3 categories relevant to the product**, screened for materiality rather than skipped wholesale
- **Emission factors with sources documented**, so a buyer's auditor can trace every number back to its basis
- A **recalculation policy**, so the numbers survive a year of operational change without starting over

## The captive-generation complication

Most Bangladeshi RMG factories run captive gas generation for reliability. That shifts a meaningful share of emissions into **Scope 1** rather than Scope 2 grid electricity — which changes both the measurement approach and the reduction strategy a buyer will expect to see. See [Why captive generation puts your emissions in Scope 1](/insights/why-captive-generation-puts-your-emissions-in-scope-1) for the detail.

## What to do before a buyer asks

Build the inventory before it's requested under deadline pressure. A base-year Scope 1–3 inventory, built to the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard with documented emission factors, is the artefact that answers a CBAM-adjacent buyer questionnaire in minutes rather than weeks.
