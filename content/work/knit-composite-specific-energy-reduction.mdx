## The situation

A vertically integrated knit composite facility — knitting, dyeing, finishing and garmenting under one roof — was facing rising buyer scrutiny on energy performance but had never had a full instrumented audit. Utility bills were tracked at the facility level only, with no breakdown by process area, so nobody could say with confidence where the energy was actually going.

## What we did

1. Installed temporary power quality analysers across the main distribution boards and each major process area (knitting, dyeing, finishing, utilities) for a full production cycle, capturing load profiles rather than relying on nameplate ratings.
2. Ran ultrasonic flow surveys on the compressed air network and thermal imaging across the dyeing and finishing lines to find losses invisible to a walkthrough.
3. Built a specific energy consumption baseline by process area, normalised to production volume, so measures could be ranked on realistic before/after impact rather than theoretical savings.
4. Costed each identified measure — compressor sequencing, insulation repair, waste heat recovery from the dyeing process, VFDs on oversized motors — and ranked the full list by payback period.

## The result

The facility began implementation with the shortest-payback measures first. The compressed air survey alone found losses equivalent to roughly a third of total compressed air generation capacity, concentrated in a small number of easily repaired leak points.
